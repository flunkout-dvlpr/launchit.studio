# Brand identity — Launchit Sessions

## Concept
Minimalist structure, maximalist personality. The grid represents the "build" —
precision, real technical work, blueprint logic. Color, texture, and playful
marks represent the "human" — community, Houston, the people whose businesses
get built. The two registers stay separate rather than blended evenly: structure
lives in layout/backgrounds/framework content, personality lives in accents,
badges, and photography.

## Color palette

**Structural (base):**
- Paper `#F4F0E6` — background, warm off-white, not sterile white
- Blueprint navy `#1E2B3C` — primary ink/text, headers
- Gridline blue `#3E7CA6` — literal grid lines, thin dividers, technical accents

**Personality (Houston accent trio — use sparingly, as accents/badges, not backgrounds):**
- Sunset coral `#D85A30` — Gulf Coast sunset
- Gulf gold `#EF9F27` — humid golden-hour light
- Bayou teal `#1D9E75` — bayou water

Rule of thumb: any given page should be 80%+ paper/navy/gridline, with the
accent trio reserved for badges, tags, callouts, and cycle-specific highlights.
If a page uses all three accents evenly across large areas, it's over-maximalist —
pull back.

## Typography

- **Structural/technical** — Space Grotesk (or similar grotesk with a technical
  feel). Used for: navigation, labels, framework/process page copy, anything
  that should read like an engineering annotation.
- **Personality/voice** — Fraunces (or similar serif with character, italic
  available). Used for: headlines, pull quotes, the "why this exists" moments.
  The contrast between grotesk-as-label and serif-as-headline is what carries
  the minimalist/maximalist tension more than any single graphic element.

Avoid a third typeface. Two is enough — a third dilutes the contrast that's
doing the work.

## Motifs
- Grid lines / graph paper texture — literal, low-opacity, as a background
  texture on structural pages (About, Framework)
- Dimension lines and annotation marks — blueprint-style measurement ticks,
  used as small decorative dividers, not literal callouts
- Rotated badge/pill elements — slightly tilted (1-4 degrees), used for tags,
  cycle numbers, category labels. This is the primary "maximalist" graphic move —
  use it for short label text only, never for body copy
- No stock tech imagery (circuit boards, robots, abstract AI visuals)
- No corporate SaaS gradients or glassmorphism

## Photography direction
Real people from actual sessions — submitters, attendees, the room. Warm,
candid, not staged corporate headshots. If Houston environmental shots are
used (venue, street, texture), keep them incidental/background, not the
hero image — the people are the subject.

## Houston iconography — the collage layer
The signature maximalist move: real Houston landmarks and murals treated as if
torn out of a magazine and physically plastered across the grid. This is what
makes the site unmistakably Houston rather than a generic blueprint template.

**Subject matter (illustrative, not exhaustive):**
- The "Be Someone" mural over I-45 downtown
- Other well-known murals and mural districts around the city
- Recognizable local spots — Buffalo Bayou, the Waugh Bridge bats, Water Wall,
  Astros/Space City nods, local taco truck and small-business signage — anything
  that reads as "this is Houston" rather than stock skyline photography

**Execution:**
- Torn/cutout edges — jagged, irregular paper-tear silhouette, not a clean
  crop. Simulated via clip-path or a torn-edge mask, not a literal rectangle.
- Slight rotation per clipping (2-6 degrees), like something pinned or taped
  down in a hurry, not perfectly aligned
- A hint of physical media — subtle drop shadow, a "tape strip" or "pin"
  accent at a corner, faint paper grain — enough to sell "cutout," not so much
  it looks like a full scrapbook effect
- Clippings sit *on top of* the grid/blueprint layer, breaking its rigidity —
  this is the literal collision point between the two halves of the brand
- Use as scattered accents (hero background, section dividers, About page
  margins), not as full-bleed backgrounds — they're punctuation, not wallpaper

**Sourcing note:** these should be real photos taken specifically for the
site (or clearly licensed), not pulled from a general image search — murals
are often original artwork with their own copyright even when in public view,
so treat photographing them the way you'd treat photographing any public art:
fine for editorial/community use, but worth crediting the mural artist where
known, and worth avoiding anything that reads as commercial reproduction of
someone else's work at large scale.

## Page-by-page application
- **Home / About** — structural-leaning: paper background, grid texture,
  gridline dividers, one or two accent badges max, plus 1-3 Houston collage
  clippings as hero/margin accents
- **Framework** — most structural page on the site; this is the "engineering
  drawing" of the whole thing, grid + annotation style diagrams work well here;
  collage clippings kept minimal or absent so the process reads clearly
- **Prep artifacts / event recaps** — most personality-leaning; accent colors,
  tilted badges, real photography from the session, and collage clippings of
  the venue/neighborhood where relevant

## Open item
Palette and type pairing above are a proposed starting direction, not final —
confirm against specific reference elements (not just portfolio titles) before
locking in for build. Revisit once 1-2 real event photos exist to test the
photography direction against actual material.
